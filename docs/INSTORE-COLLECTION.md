# In-store collection — separate physical goods, POS-only cash

**Deployment status:** code and source files are on this branch for review. The installed Contabo **live pilot remains read-only** (last locally checked as 1.1.6); this feature does not enable trading on it. The standalone live-trading service template is **not installed** and also forces the new in-store cash gate off. The public HTTPS `/api/ready` result requested after the earlier pilot restart has not been reported. Do not repurpose the pilot ledger, service, Woo key or port. Do not interpret a green catalogue check as cash-sale acceptance.

## Which stock and prices?

- `products.csv` supplies the client-approved **final all-in unit amount in GHS**. It has no stock quantities and no usable tax percentage. Do **not** infer VAT, discount, payment processing, WooCommerce IDs or online stock from this CSV. A payable amount must already have **at most two decimal places**; do not silently round a fraction of a pesewa.
- `Stock-2026-11-12.pdf` supplies physically checked, POS-exclusive quantities, as confirmed by the client. `data/instore-stock-snapshot.json` records a reviewed PDF extraction with the source-file SHA-256 hashes, all numbered report rows, SKU quantities/units and one SKU-less report row. Five quantity digits obscured by missing unit text were confirmed visually and recorded in the reviewed snapshot; a fresh script extraction leaves those quantities unknown until a human checks the new PDF. Its SHA-256 is `6e99296585cfa4c475f9a205ca7830a424439d115e30a387298457f38ea35973`. `scripts/extract-instore-stock.py` needs `pypdf` **only when reviewing a replacement PDF**; no PDF parser or Python runtime is required by the deployed POS. A new snapshot must be manually reviewed and pinned before any new import; never overwrite trading stock by rerunning extraction. Native deployments must keep all three source files beside the server code; the Docker runtime image packages them **outside `dist/`**, and CI verifies the original hashes inside that image.
- The PDF has **1,571 numbered rows**. All **1,570 populated unique CSV SKUs** occur once in it, plus a matching SKU-less entry on **report row 508 / PDF page 17** (two reported units). Of the 1,570 coded rows, quantities read as **1,215 positive, 327 zero, 28 negative**. Five rows initially lacked machine-parsed quantities but were visually checked: their numbers are legible, yet they have **no unit of measure in either source** (four positive and one zero); another four have malformed units. Three prices (`1469`, `1470`, `1471`) are `194.1177` GHS and need an explicit owner-approved payable-cent amount. The SKU-less row, 28 negative counts, nine unit exceptions and three fractional prices are **41 distinct quarantined entries**. The remaining **1,530** are eligible for a first import: **1,206 positive** and **324 zero**. These counts are *product records*, not unit totals.
- Specifically, blank-unit SKUs are `632`, `1202`, `698`, `697`, `696`; malformed-unit SKUs are `100` (`Eacg`) and `139`, `140`, `141` (`Eachb`). The PDF visibly reports quantities for the five blank-unit rows, but the program must not assume their unit is a sellable "Each". Negative counts stay negative for review and are **not** converted to zero or backordered. Three of the 327 zero-stock rows are quarantined for missing/malformed units.
- The client explicitly approved publishing the original PDF and CSV **including costs and quantities** in this public repository. The authenticated POS catalogue and receipts deliberately expose **no cost/markup fields**. Do not copy source-cost columns into a sale or export. The original files are public by explicit approval; keep costs out of the POS API and receipts.

## Operational separation and cash accounting

The **In-store collection** sidebar page is not the WooCommerce Point of sale, Inventory, Sales history, Wholesale or Reports page. When locked, it shows searched/filterable goods, report quantities, status, prices and 41 review reasons but **offers no add-to-cart, cash sale or stock adjustment**. Its `GET /api/instore/catalog` requires a signed-in live user, validates the source hashes and does not import stock or call WooCommerce. Even if Woo catalogue refresh fails, the in-store section can load independently.

Only after a *separate* owner-reviewed rollout can a **different live trading ledger** import the eligible, immutable source once and offer the in-store cash cart:

1. A distinct `POS_INSTORE_CASH_APPROVED=true`, an in-store acceptance reference (at least eight characters, not the Woo Retail approval), the **exact** `POS_INSTORE_SOURCE_SHA256` above, `POS_MODE=live`, `POS_LIVE_READ_ONLY=false`, and a mandatory POS shift must all be present. The **pilot unit pins the gate off** and read-only mode independently rejects it. The **not-installed trading unit also pins the gate off** until its unit and tests receive a separate reviewed amendment. Woo Retail and Wholesale write approvals never imply this flag; conversely, in-store cash does not grant Woo writes. The template remains locked today.
2. At approved first startup, the new private SQLite ledger imports only the 1,530 eligible SKU identities, final cent prices and nonnegative quantities in one transaction, with a source fingerprint and import movements. Later starts verify the fingerprint and **never reseed** sold stock. A changed source stops checkout until the owner reviews a controlled migration; simply replacing the CSV/PDF or editing their hash cannot replenish inventory.
3. Cashiers must have an open POS shift. The client sends SKU/quantity, the amount it expects to collect, the cash already counted and a UUID attempt key; it **cannot send a payment method, discounted price, tax amount or Woo ID**. The server rechecks source, eligible identity, current POS balance, exact price/amount, shift and cash in `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; a paid numbered receipt, sale record, stock deductions, movements and audit event **commit together** or all roll back. Distinct attempts cannot oversell the last unit. Retries with the same key return the first receipt; changed cart/cash with that key is refused.
4. A lost response is **not** permission to take payment again. The browser saves the attempt key before submission and can look up the original receipt or retry **that exact attempt**. Before discarding a verified-absent attempt, an owner must handle any cash already taken. The owner alone may correct the physical **count of an already eligible SKU**, with a reason, expected previous balance, unique key and movement/audit entry. This never clears a quarantined code or changes website stock.
5. In-store receipts show **final gross GHS lines/total, cash tendered and change**, with no itemized or zero-rated tax claim, as explicitly accepted by the client. They have their own `IS-...` number and printable 58/80 mm/A4 layouts. Paid cash receipts/history, owner/manager reports and formula-safe CSV export are **separate** from Woo Retail/Wholesale. The POS drawer's expected cash/close-of-shift summary includes both pools and identifies the in-store subset; it does not double-count it. In-store sales do **not** create WooCommerce orders, adjust online stock or touch Paystack.

**Not implemented:** online checkout for physical goods, MoMo/card/Paystack in this pool, refunds/returns/voiding of in-store receipts, automatic replenishment/import of a new snapshot, identity or price fixes for the 41 exceptions, and a tax-invoice breakdown. An owner must establish a separate, legally appropriate refund/return procedure before selling, and arrange accountable price/identity corrections before any quarantined item can enter a newly reviewed source. A count correction cannot be used as a substitute.

## Before any real in-store cash rollout

- Obtain an **explicit in-store acceptance plan/reference** distinct from Woo order approvals. Check the PDF counts really are exclusive to this POS at first sale and that there is no competing till, unsynced purchase or online stock dependency. Reconcile the 41 exceptions on paper; do **not** unblock them by manually editing a trading DB. Resolve the three fractional-cent prices with the owner before selling those SKUs.
- Verify owner-approved business/receipt details, tax/compliance requirements for a gross-only receipt, physical printer/paper and drawer, cash handling, named staff permissions, end-of-shift Z report, and an actual supervised **genuine** cash sale (not dummy payment). Test response-loss recovery and a second cashier trying the last unit in isolated fixtures, then use a controlled real observation if approved.
- Establish a separately backed-up and **off-host restorable** POS trading ledger. Test a safe restore on a different directory and rehearse emergency lock: turn only this in-store gate off, preserve the ledger/receipts and reconcile physical cash; never restore over newer transactions. Source hashes, printed receipts and stock movements must remain available. Woo website and the other VPS app must remain untouched.
- Review any separate Nginx upstream change and systemd amendment/test **before** installing/activating a trading service. Keep the pilot at `127.0.0.1:3187`, read-only with its Read-only Woo key. The uninstalled trading template on `3188` currently remains locked; do not enable it by editing only an env file. There is no instruction here to create a Woo Read/Write key or enable Woo Retail/Wholesale writes.

For isolation tests, run `npm test`. The browser suite (`npm run test:browser`) uses an **isolated demo service with intercepted live-mode UI responses**, not real goods/money or a production connection.
