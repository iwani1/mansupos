## Intended boundaries

- **One authority:** WooCommerce owns shared product IDs/SKUs, names, website retail prices and managed stock. The POS mirrors the published Woo catalogue while open (currently refreshes roughly every minute when visible) and rechecks remotely before checkout. A cached or historical PDF figure must never authorize a sale. Woo unavailable/unknown stock means no shared-stock POS sale. A display refresh is **not** an atomic stock reservation; concurrent last-unit behaviour needs separate Woo-side verification/safeguards.
- **Online retail:** Woo/Paystack checkout remains unchanged; only Woo's verified order-status/stock rules decide when an online purchase reduces availability. Do not add an extra POS decrement or infer stock from Woo emails alone.
- **Shop wholesale:** Build on the existing Woo-backed Wholesale quote workflow, with owner/manager approval, a negotiated final tax-inclusive unit price **for one sale only**, a cash shift and a reconciled Woo order. Never change the product's retail website price to the wholesale price. Actual pending/paid notifications, tax calculation, last-item safety and loss-of-response recovery require real-store acceptance before taking cash. No direct POS Paystack or offline payment queue.
- **No double pool:** The current `In-store collection` source snapshot and POS-only sale/stock-count routes are **legacy for this target**. Do not import its 1,530 eligible items into the POS-only ledger, record an `IS-...` sale for these goods, or present its PDF counts as live shared stock. Retain historical test coverage, but leave all production gates locked until a reviewed Woo-backed cutover. The trading service is not installed.


The tool needs **no Woo key or raw Woo export**. It writes two SHA-named Markdown files under ignored `test-results/`: a short preview to discuss and a full detail list. Both are file mode 0600, contain no per-item price/quantity, import-ready CSV, Woo ID or stock setting, and do not overwrite prior owner notes. Even a clean-looking group is **only a proposal**; an owner must verify the goods and decide whether colour should also be a Woo variation.
