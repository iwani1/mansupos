# Tax decision — questions for the tax adviser

**Status:** Open. WooCommerce tax is currently **switched off**, so POS receipts show no tax. That is only correct if the business is not required to charge tax. This page is a checklist to take to a tax adviser; it is not tax advice.

## Questions to ask

1. Is the business required to register for and charge **VAT** at its current annual turnover? What is the threshold, and how close are we?
2. Which **levies** apply on top of VAT (for example NHIL, GETFund Levy, COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy), and at what rates today?
3. Are prices on the website and shelf **tax-inclusive** or tax-exclusive? (Retail customers usually expect inclusive prices.)
4. Does GRA's **E-VAT** electronic invoicing apply to us? If so, which certified invoicing route must the POS and the website use, and by when?
5. What must a **receipt** show (TIN, business name and address, invoice number, tax breakdown)?
6. Do online orders and in-shop orders get the same treatment?
7. How long must sales records be kept, and in what form?

Bring: last 12 months' sales totals (POS Reports → CSV export, plus WooCommerce reports), the business registration and TIN.

## After the answer

**If no tax is due:** record the adviser's answer and date here, keep tax off, and set a reminder to recheck when turnover grows.

**If tax is due:**

1. In WooCommerce → Settings → General, enable taxes; under the **Tax** tab set prices inclusive/exclusive as advised and add the standard rate(s).
2. Do this first on a copy or outside shop hours. In the POS, make one test sale and check that the WooCommerce total, the tax line and the receipt agree with the adviser's example.
3. The POS follows WooCommerce's tax calculation; it does not compute tax itself. If E-VAT applies, POS changes are needed — plan them as a separate piece of work.
